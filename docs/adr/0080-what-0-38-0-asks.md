# ADR 0080: What guideline 0.38.0 asks

**Status**: accepted (2026-09-29).

## Context

Tadas moves its pin from guideline v0.37.0 to v0.38.0. The release
changes no rule of `architecture.md` and no lens. It bounds every loop
an agent runs in the ops skill templates by a count, well above real
use, and says what the skill does when the count is reached. Money and
time still bound each loop first. Here is where Tadas stands on each
text the release changes:

| Text | What v0.38.0 asks | Here |
|------|-------------------|------|
| `ops-watch` | At most 30 batches. An interval of 30 seconds to five minutes; a window of more than 30 intervals widens it to the window divided by 30, so an hour asked at 10 seconds runs 30 batches of two minutes. At most 20 tool calls a batch; a read that would be the 21st is written as not read. The `--period` passed to `get-metric-data` is a whole minute. The sub-agent names its Next and never runs it. | Follows with this change. A batch's main path is six calls in the cloud: the wait, the identity check, the `api` and `maintenance` log reads, the alarms, and one `get-metric-data`. A window longer than 30 batches of five minutes stops at the 30th, and the report names the part not watched. A batch passes its start and end rounded down to whole minutes, and the period is 60 seconds for any interval, so no minute is read twice: the template's period, the interval rounded down to a multiple of 60, would reach past a batch's rounded end into the next batch's minutes. Locally, each signal is `increase(<metric>[1m])` over the same minutes, at a 60-second `step`. |
| `ops-root-cause` | At most 5 request ids, one pass each, and each id past the fifth listed as not followed. A pass that finds no cause reports "not found". The events feed is read from the window's first `seq`, found by probing `after_seq` with `limit=1`. | Follows with this change. `--request-id` may be given more than once. The probe dates an event by its `produced_at`, since the operator's feed publishes no `id` ([ADR 0015](0015-the-event-keeps-its-produced-at.md)). |
| `ops-investigate`, `ops-root-cause` | A Logs Insights query is polled at most 10 times, `sleep 5` apart, then reported as not read with its query id. | Follows with this change. Both skills allow `sleep`. `ops-investigate` polls its providers' query the same way. `tadas-ops signals check` already bounds its own poll in code: 30 polls a second apart. |
| `ops-investigate`, `ops-watch`, `stress-test-run` | A session follows at most 2 hops of Next; the report of the second hop names its next skill, and the session stops there. | Follows with this change. |
| `ops-cloud-deployment-create`, `stress-test-create-or-update` | Their Next is the person's to run, never the session's. | Follows with this change. |
| `audit-database-calls` | A failed flow is fixed and run again once: the first run plus at most 1 rerun. | Follows with this change. The skill already ran a fixed flow once, into `calls_2.json`. It now says that is the count, and that a flow failing again is reported as not measured. |

The rest of the release changes the guideline's scaffold skills, its
`arch-reviewer` agent, its own gates, and its benchmark. None of them
is a text in this tree.

`make check` runs `arch-check` at v0.38.0, and it passes: 106 rules
over 352 files, no finding, and 7 accepted by an exception. The five
entries of `[[tool.arch-check.exception]]` in `pyproject.toml` accept
those seven between them; ADR 0079's "5" counted the entries. The
checker of v0.38.0 is the checker of v0.37.0 with a new version number,
so it finds the same.

## Decision

**Each skill keeps its own text and gains the bound in its own words.**
Tadas's skills are fuller than the templates. Each bound goes into the
step it bounds, with what the skill does when it is reached, and the
"What it never does" list repeats it. Nothing the skill already said is
removed.

**A pass reads each signal once, one way.** `ops-root-cause` reads a
request's signals through its own steps or through `tadas-ops signals
check` in one call. A pass takes one of the two, never both, so the
one-read bound holds whichever it takes.

**A test holds each count.** `infra/tests/test_ops_skills.py` names
each bound's sentence, so a rewrite that drops one fails `make check`.
It also holds that the two skills whose Next is the person's count no
hops, and that the three that wait may run `sleep`.

**Nothing of v0.38.0 is a deviation.**

## Consequences

- A new loop in an ops skill states its count and what the skill does
  at it, and joins `COUNT_BOUNDS` in the test.
- A session that follows a report's Next counts its hops, and stops at
  the second.
- `ops-root-cause` reads the events feed from the window's first `seq`,
  and no page from before it.
